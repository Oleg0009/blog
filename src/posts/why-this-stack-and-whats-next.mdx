# Why This Stack, and What I Want to Learn Next

## Why these technologies

Honestly, the stack was mostly a recommendation from an AI assistant, so I didn't
overthink it. I chose it because it was the easiest way for me to start — to start
progressing, to write down my thoughts, and to improve my skills. Sometimes the best
choice is just the one that lets you begin.

## Why a blog

A blog is one of those things that can train your brain and prepare it to think in a more
creative way — maybe that's the real reason I started. I also want this to work like a
diary: a place to write down all my thoughts and ideas, the ones that could be
implemented in the future. Putting them into words might even help me structure my
thinking.

## What I want to improve

The point of this post is to actually start improving. And before you write something,
you usually need to read something.

Right now I want to grow in a few directions:

- **AI and agentic workflows** — so much work now happens with the help of AI, and I
  think the newest skills are opening up in that space.
- **E-commerce** — a deeper understanding of how it all works, including platforms like
  BigCommerce.
- **Performance optimization** — Core Web Vitals, browser tricks, and the lower-level
  things a developer at my level should really know.

## Where I'm starting from

For context: I have 6+ years of development experience — Next.js, React, Vue, Shopify,
Liquid, and several headless CMS platforms (Amplience, Contentstack, Contentful). I've
already rebuilt some projects with Claude Code as full AI migrations. I'm also working on
a certification right now — trying to pass the Claude Architect (Foundation) exam.

## The honest part

The truth is, I don't know exactly what I need to become a stronger developer. But I don't
want to stop, and I want to grow. That's the whole idea behind starting this: I want to be
better and know more than other developers on the market, so I can stay competitive.

That's all for today. Thanks for reading.
